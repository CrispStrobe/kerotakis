# FeCl2+ visible absorptivity: Connick & Coppel lead read (2026-11-20)

FeCl2+ (the monochloro iron(III) complex, written FeCl++ in the older papers) still has no sourced
visible absorptivity, and the engine keeps refusing its colour. This note records the lead that
was checked.

## Read: Connick & Coppel, UCRL-8629 (1959)

R. E. Connick and C. P. Coppel, *Kinetics of the Formation of the Ferric Chloride Complex*,
UCRL-8629, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, 1 February 1959,
https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2tv7q2n2. The section "Molar Extinction Coefficients", Table 4,
gives approximate epsilon values for Fe3+, FeOH2+, Fe2(OH)2 4+ and FeCl2+. These are:

- tabulated at 300-390 nm only, measured on a Beckman DU in 2 cm cells at 25 ± 0.1 C, ionic
  strength 1.15 (perchlorate), 0.250 M H+, with 0.0555 M NaCl;
- for FeCl2+: 2.?8 x 10^2 L/mol/cm at 390 nm (one digit is illegible in the scan), 4.28 x 10^2 at
  380 nm and 7.02 x 10^2 at 370 nm; the rows below 370 nm were not legible in the text layer;
- explicitly approximate: "the individual values may well be in error by ten percent".

The report says why it stops at 390 nm: "Because of the low absorption of FeCl++ above 400 mμ it
was necessary to work between 300 and 400 mμ". It gives no value in the renderer's bands
(405-705 nm). It uses 570 nm only as a wavelength where hydrolysed iron absorbs little, and gives
no FeCl2+ epsilon there.

The related UCRL-8059 thesis (C. P. Coppel, UNT digital library ark:/67531/metadc1019065) has the
same Part III section. The UNT server refused connections on 2026-11-20, so it was not read.

## Still unread

Rabinowitch & Stockmayer, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 64 (1942) 335, which Connick & Coppel cite as the
spectrophotometric study above 400 nm. It is behind a paywall and was not available here.

## Outcome

There is no tabulated FeCl2+ epsilon at 405 nm or above. The 370-390 nm values lie outside the
16 renderer bands. Extrapolating them into the visible would invent data, so nothing was added
and the FeCl2+ colour refusal stays.
